# Send to an agent or publisher

Agents and editors ask for a plain manuscript, not a designed book. The
**Agent or publisher** tab makes one in Standard Manuscript Format. The text
is double-spaced, and the first page has your contact details and the word
count.

## Make the manuscript

1. Open your [author profile](./characters-and-authors). Check that it has
   your **Name**, postal address, email and phone. The first page needs them.
   **Name** is your legal name; **Pen name / byline** is the name readers see.
2. Open the project's **Export** page.
3. Choose **Agent or publisher**. If a contact detail is missing, the tab
   tells you which one. **Open the author profile** takes you to the profile.
4. Choose the **Manuscript typeface**: **Times New Roman** or **Courier**.
5. Choose the **Paper**: **Letter** or **A4**.
6. Press **Export Word**. If the agent asks for a PDF, press **Export PDF**.

Read each agent's guidelines before you send. Most agents want Word, a few
want PDF, and some ask for only the first chapters.

## What the manuscript looks like

- **First page:** your name and contact details are at the top left. The word
  count is at the top right; it is rounded, as agents expect. The title and
  your byline are halfway down the page.
- **The other pages:** 12 pt type, double-spaced, with one-inch margins. Each
  paragraph is indented half an inch. "Surname / KEYWORD / page" is at the top
  right. The keyword is the first main word of the title, in capitals: "The
  Weighing House" gives WEIGHING.
- **Chapters** start on a new page, a third of the way down.
- **Scene breaks** are a centred `#`. The manuscript ends with the word END.
- **Italics stay italics.** Underlining italics is an old rule from
  typewriters. Editors today read italics.
- **No cover, no separate title page and no front matter.** The manuscript
  doesn't use the book's design either. It is plain so that an editor can
  mark it up.
