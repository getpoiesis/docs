# Send to an agent or publisher

Agents and editors ask for a plain typescript, not a typeset book. The
**Agent or publisher** tab makes exactly that: Standard Manuscript Format,
double-spaced, with your contact details and the word count on the first
page.

## Make the manuscript

1. Make sure your [author profile](./characters-and-authors) has your
   **Name** (your legal name; **Pen name / byline** is what readers see), postal
   address, email and phone. The first page needs them, and the
   tab tells you which are missing (**Open the author profile** takes you
   there).
2. Open the project's **Export** page and choose **Agent or publisher**.
3. Choose the **Manuscript typeface**, **Times New Roman** or **Courier**,
   and the **Paper**, **Letter** or **A4**.
4. Press **Export Word**, or **Export PDF** for agents who ask for one.

Check each agent's own guidelines before you send: most want Word, a few want
PDF, and some ask for only the first chapters.

## What the manuscript looks like

- **First page:** your name and contact details at the top left, the word
  count (rounded, as agents expect) at the top right, then the title and your
  byline halfway down the page.
- **The other pages:** 12 pt, double-spaced, one-inch margins, paragraphs
  indented half an inch, and "Surname / KEYWORD / page" at the top right. The
  keyword is the title's first main word, in capitals ("The Salt Road" gives
  SALT).
- **Chapters** start on a new page, a third of the way down.
- **Scene breaks** are a centred `#`, and the manuscript closes with END.
- **Italics stay italics.** The old rule of underlining them comes from
  typewriters; editors today read italics.
- **No cover, no title page of its own, and no front matter.** None of the
  book's design, either: a manuscript is meant to be marked up.
